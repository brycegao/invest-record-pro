# invest-record-pro Market And Positioning Analysis

## Summary

invest-record-pro has promising potential, but it is not a short-term explosive trend product. It is better understood as a small, focused, high-retention vertical tool with clear long-term value.

For the project owner, it is highly worth building because it has both self-use value and possible commercialization potential.

> Note: Market size figures in this document are working assumptions and should be verified before being used in external-facing materials.

## 1. Market Demand

### 1.1 Market Size

Working assumptions:

- Global personal finance management software market: around USD 123 billion in 2024, expected to reach around USD 195 billion in 2033, CAGR around 6.5%.
- China bookkeeping and personal finance software market: expected to reach around RMB 5.46 billion in 2026, growth around 12.3%.
- Investment management is a high-value subcategory of personal finance management, assumed to account for around 18% and grow faster than the broader category.

These figures need source verification before external publication.

### 1.2 Strong User Pain Points

Retail investors have three core pain points:

- No decision record: buy and sell decisions are often based on feeling, and users cannot explain why they made a decision afterwards.
- Poor discipline execution: greed, fear, and hesitation often override the original plan.
- No structured review: users focus only on profit and loss, not on decision quality or repeatable rules.

invest-record-pro directly targets these pain points by focusing on plan, execution, position, and review.

### 1.3 Clear Target Users

Core users:

- A-share and ETF individual investors.
- Age range around 30-50.
- Users with meaningful investable assets.
- Users who care about investment discipline and long-term decision quality.

Extended users:

- Fund investment users.
- Hong Kong and US stock retail investors.
- More active traders who need trade journals and review tools.

Trend:

- Financial literacy is improving.
- Market volatility increases demand for discipline tools.
- Some users are shifting from speculative trading to more rational investing.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

### 2.1 Brokerage And Market Data Apps

Examples:

- East Money.
- Tonghuashun.
- Huatai/ZhangLe Fortune Path.

Advantages:

- Comprehensive market data.
- Convenient trading.
- Rich news and information.

Weaknesses:

- Lack of a plan-execution-review loop.
- Focus on watching markets rather than recording decision quality.
- Financial data is usually cloud-based.
- AI features tend to focus on information, marketing, or recommendations, rather than behavior attribution and discipline review.

### 2.2 General Bookkeeping Apps

Examples:

- Suishouji.
- Wacai.

Advantages:

- Simple.
- Free or low cost.
- Large user base.

Weaknesses:

- Weak investment scenario support.
- No asset thesis management.
- No plan template.
- No position and strategy discipline model.
- No structured investment review.

### 2.3 Niche Trade Journal Tools

Examples:

- TraderVue.
- Edgewonk.
- TurtleTrace.

Advantages:

- Stronger in trading journals and review.
- Some products have better privacy and local workflows.

Weaknesses:

- Usually focus only on trade logs.
- Weak plan management.
- Weak dynamic position tracking.
- Weak AI support.
- Poor China market adaptation, such as A-share indices, RMB, Chinese templates, and domestic ETF scenarios.

### 2.4 Competitive Conclusion

invest-record-pro's positioning has few direct competitors:

> Local privacy + full investment decision loop + AI behavior analysis.

The product should not compete head-on with market data apps or brokerage apps. Its unique value is disciplined decision recording and post-trade review.

## 3. Core Differentiation

### 3.1 Privacy First

Local-first storage and local AI can address users' concerns about sensitive financial data leakage.

Potential differentiation:

- Local database.
- No mandatory cloud account.
- Data does not leave the device by default.
- Optional data desensitization.
- Local model support through tools such as Ollama.

This is especially important for users with larger assets or strong privacy concerns.

### 3.2 Full Decision Loop

The product should record the complete investment loop:

```text
Asset
-> Plan
-> Trade execution
-> Position change
-> Review
-> Rule improvement
```

The key metric should not only be profit and loss, but also:

- Plan execution rate.
- Impulsive trade count.
- Rule violation count.
- Review completion rate.
- Position discipline.

### 3.3 Practical AI Positioning

The AI feature should avoid stock recommendation and short-term market prediction.

Better AI tasks:

- Convert natural-language plans into structured rules.
- Check plan consistency.
- Identify position and risk issues.
- Summarize emotional trading patterns.
- Generate monthly discipline reports.
- Draft post-trade reviews.
- Help refine user rules over time.

This positioning is more practical and has lower compliance risk than recommendation-oriented AI.

### 3.4 China Retail Investor Adaptation

invest-record-pro should deeply adapt to China retail investor scenarios:

- A-shares.
- ETFs.
- Mutual funds and QDII funds.
- Bonds and cash-like assets.
- Shanghai Composite Index.
- CSI 300.
- SSE 50.
- RMB.
- Chinese review templates.
- Manual data entry first.
- Later import from broker statements if compliant and technically feasible.

## 4. Risks

### 4.1 Market Size And Willingness To Pay

The product is valuable, but it may remain a niche tool. Users who truly care about discipline and review are a subset of investors.

Mitigation:

- Start as a self-use product.
- Build a high-quality local-first tool.
- Validate with a small number of serious users before broad commercialization.

### 4.2 Data Entry Friction

Manual trade recording can become a burden.

Mitigation:

- Make entry fast.
- Support templates.
- Support CSV import.
- Later consider broker statement import.

### 4.3 AI Quality

Local models may produce weaker summaries than cloud models.

Mitigation:

- Use structured aggregation before calling AI.
- Keep prompts narrow.
- Make AI output editable.
- Save prompt versions and input snapshots.

### 4.4 Compliance Boundary

The product should avoid becoming an investment advisory or stock recommendation tool.

Mitigation:

- Position as a decision record and review tool.
- Avoid deterministic buy/sell recommendations.
- Make AI focus on behavior, discipline, and risk review.

## 5. Landing Path

### 5.1 MVP Focus

First version should focus on:

- Asset management.
- Buy/sell plans.
- Trade records.
- Position summary.
- Review records.
- Local AI monthly summary.

### 5.2 Product Principle

Build a tool that helps users answer:

```text
Why did I buy?
Did I follow my plan?
What should I improve next time?
```

### 5.3 Commercialization Possibilities

Potential future models:

- Paid local desktop app.
- Pro version with more templates and reports.
- One-time license.
- Paid import/export and advanced analytics.
- Privacy-first AI investment journal for serious retail investors.

Commercialization should come after validating retention and self-use value.
